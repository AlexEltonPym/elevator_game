# Ablation report — express-acceleration model & mechanic restricted-play

Hand-written (unlike `docs/depth-report.md`, which is regenerated by the depth
run). Covers the two ablations of `docs/v4-spec.md`: the express-acceleration
model choice, and the restricted-play study of what each mechanic is worth.

**Method.** Every number below is a win/lose COUNT over the 16 held-in
`Scenarios3.SEEDS_TUNE` seeds, produced by
`tools/run_depth.gd -- --tune [--accel MODEL] [--restrict WHAT]`. The
hand-tuned **thesis** is used as the best-achievable-score proxy: the depth
report shows the thesis sits at or within noise of the EA optimum on every
level, so "does the thesis still win / does the naive still lose" is the
cleanest read on whether a mechanic is load-bearing. `--tune` never touches the
held-out `SEEDS_ASSERT`, which stays the regression gate (`tests/run_balance.gd`).

The two knobs are `Levels3.accel_model` and `Levels3.restrict`, both defaulting
to the shipped behaviour and both settable from the depth CLI, so nothing here
changes the shipped game.

---

## A. Express acceleration: accel-by-width vs accel-by-speed-class

The shipped model (`accel_model = "width"`) derives a car's ramp from its
width, so a width-2 express (520 px/s) and a width-2 standard (260 px/s) share
the same `accel` (300). Over a single 90 px cell neither reaches top speed
(`sqrt(2·300·90) = 232 px/s`), so **they are indistinguishable over one hop** —
the express only pulls ahead on a long uninterrupted leg where it cruises at
520. That is the legibility complaint the spec raised.

Two alternatives derive the ramp from top speed instead (`card_accel`):

| model | express accel | standard | pod | cargo | express identity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **width** (shipped) | 300 | 300 | 560 | 160 | only on long legs |
| **speed_fast** (faster ramps faster) | 600 | 300 | 392 | 231 | distinct even over one cell |
| **speed_slow** (faster ramps slower) | 150 | 300 | 229 | 390 | sluggish off the line, fast at cruise |

### Axiom survival under each model (thesis WINS / naive LOSES over 16 tune seeds)

| level | width (shipped) | speed_fast | speed_slow |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 Tower   | 16 / 16 | 16 / 16 | 16 / 16 |
| L2 Detour  | 16 / 16 | 16 / 16 | 16 / 16 |
| L4 Ring    | 16 / 16 | 16 / 16 | **4** / 16 |
| W-3 Momentum | 16 / 16 | 16 / 16 | 16 / 16 (naive served ~55 vs ~14 — margin halved) |

### Decision: **keep accel-by-width.**

- **speed_slow is strictly worse.** It breaks L4 (the express, ramping at 150,
  is crippled on the stop-heavy ring weave, so the thesis falls to 4/16), and
  it inverts the cargo's feel — a heavy freight car ramping at 390 (snappier
  than a standard) reads wrong.
- **speed_fast ties accel-by-width on axiom survival** (no level breaks) and
  genuinely gives the express a more legible ramp — it out-accelerates a
  standard even over one cell, which is exactly the "return L2 to its corridor,
  not its cabin" gain the spec hoped for. But it shows **no measured depth
  advantage**: the win-margins are identical to width, and adopting it would
  force a full re-tune of every level (the spec's own retuning warning) for a
  purely qualitative gain.
- Per the spec's explicit tiebreak — *"if it's close, keep accel-by-width
  (simpler) and record that"* — width wins. **speed_fast is the recommended
  alternative** should a future pass want express legibility and accept the
  retune; it is one flag away (`Levels3.accel_model = "speed_fast"`).

*(Caveat: skill_gap under the full EA was not re-measured per model — three
models × eight levels of 6400-eval search was outside this pass's compute
budget. The evidence here is axiom-survival and win-margin over the tune seeds,
which is sufficient to reject speed_slow and to invoke the "close → keep width"
rule between speed_fast and width.)*

## B. Restricted play: what is each mechanic worth?

### Capacity — `--restrict capacity` (every car cut to capacity = width, i.e. HALF the shipped 2·width)

| level | thesis WINS: full cap → half cap |
|---|---|
| L1 Tower    | 16 → **0** |
| L2 Detour   | 16 → **0** |
| L3 Junction | 16 → **0** |
| L4 Ring     | 16 → **0** |
| W-2 Narrows | 16 → **0** |
| W-1 Freight | 16 → **16** |
| W-3 Momentum| 16 → **14** |

**Verdict: capacity is REAL, not chrome — the on-record prediction is refuted
for the suite.** Halving capacity collapses five of seven levels from a clean
win to a total loss: on L1–L4 and W-2 the shipped cabin sizes (L2's cap-8
express, L3's cap-6 feeders, the width-2 workhorses) are load-bearing throughput.
Capacity only measures as chrome on the two levels whose bottleneck is a
*different* mechanic — **W-1** (its axiom is width-exclusivity + dedicating the
cargo car, and the freight shuttle wins even at cargo cap-3) and **W-3** (a
momentum level; capacity is almost irrelevant, 16 → 14). So the honest reading
is *conditional*: capacity is a genuine constraint wherever the level makes
throughput the bottleneck, and surplus capacity is chrome only where
width-exclusivity or momentum is the bottleneck instead.

### Acceleration / momentum — `--restrict accel` (cars snap to top speed, the pre-v4 model)

| level | thesis WINS / naive LOSES: with accel → without |
|---|---|
| L1 Tower    | 16/16 → 16/16 |
| L2 Detour   | 16/16 → 16/16 |
| L4 Ring     | 16/16 → 16/16 |
| W-1 Freight | 16/16 → 16/**14** |
| L3 Junction | 16/16 → 16/**6**  (naive WINS 10/16) |
| W-2 Narrows | 16/16 → 16/**2**  (naive WINS 14/16) |
| W-3 Momentum| 16/16 → 16/16 |

**Verdict: acceleration is real, but it bites over LONG RUNS and at GATES, not
per-stop — and least of all on the level built to showcase it.** Removing
momentum breaks the axioms of **L3** (naive's winding loft climbs, and the
queue at the single-file loft gate, both get much cheaper — naive wins 10/16)
and **W-2** (naive's long perimeter laps get fast enough to serve the middle
rush — naive wins 14/16). Both are levels whose theses turn on *long
uninterrupted travel or gate contention*, exactly where the spec says a heavy
car's momentum should tell.

The striking result is **W-3**, the dedicated momentum level: its axiom is
unmoved by removing acceleration (naive still loses 16/16). The reason is
arithmetic — on this engine per-stop momentum loss for the cargo is
`speed·(1/2accel + 1/2decel) ≈ 200·(1/320 + 1/400) ≈ 1.1 s`, which is *smaller*
than the fixed 1.8 s door dwell. So a stop-count difference (W-3's naive vs
thesis) is dominated by door time, and momentum is only a secondary amplifier
there. Momentum becomes decisive only when it accumulates over a long leg or a
gate queue (L3, W-2), never per-stop. This is worth stating plainly: **the
"momentum" mechanic earns its keep on long-leg and gate levels, and W-3 is
really a door-time-plus-momentum level, not a pure momentum isolation** (no pure
isolation is reachable while door dwell > per-stop momentum).

### Home floor (#29) — measured on W-4, its purpose-built level

W-4 "Home" was built to be the honest test of #29: naive and thesis are the
**identical** three-ferry routes, differing only in whether a home cell is set
at the station. Across three tunings:

| W-4 tuning | thesis (home) WINS | naive (no home) WINS | home delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| loose (8-floor tower)      | 9/16 | 9/16 | **0** |
| near-critical (roof shuttle, quota 68) | 4/16 | 2/16 | **+2** |
| tight | 0/16 | 0/16 | **0** |

**Verdict: the home floor is CHROME.** At its most sensitive tuning the home
cell is worth **+2 of 16 seeds**, and at every other tuning it is worth nothing
— never within reach of the axiom bar (thesis WINS 15/16 while naive LOSES
15/16). The mechanic only affects an idle car's *deadhead position*, a small
one-off latency that is swamped either by slack (both win) or by a throughput
ceiling (both lose); there is no regime where it flips the level. No other
shipped level's thesis benefits from a home, because their cars are demand-bound
(always working), not idle-position-bound.

Per the spec, **W-4 was cut** rather than fudged, and **#29 home floor is
recommended for removal from the level roster.** The `Car3.home_cell` /
`set_home_cell` / `toggle_home` code is correct and still covered by the v4
balance checks — it simply does not earn a level.

## C. Summary: which v4 mechanics are real?

| mechanic | verdict | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **width / exclusivity (#17, #1)** | **real** | W-1 (freight, capacity-independent) and W-2 (pod-only narrows) both stand on width; corridor-rejection UX ships |
| **capacity (2·width)** | **real where throughput is the bottleneck**, chrome where width/momentum is | halving it collapses 5/7 levels; chrome on W-1 & W-3 |
| **acceleration / momentum (#24)** | **real over long legs & at gates**, secondary per-stop | removing it breaks L3 & W-2; leaves W-3 (per-stop) untouched |
| **home floor (#29)** | **CHROME — cut** | ≤ +2/16 on its own purpose-built level; nil elsewhere |
